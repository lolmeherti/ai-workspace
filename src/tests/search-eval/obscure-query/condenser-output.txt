### Retractable Tape Measure History

**Key Inventors and Patent Milestones**
* **1821:** **James Chesterman** (British metalworker) is credited with inventing the first retractable tape measure, featuring a spring-loaded cloth strip in a compact case. He later patented the first steel tape measure. Early English versions were quite expensive, costing approximately $17 (over $400 in today’s currency), which drove American inventors to seek more affordable alternatives.
* **December 6, 1864:** **William H. Bangs, Jr.** (of Meriden, Connecticut) received a patent for the first American design of a spring-return (retractable) tape measure.
* **July 14, 1868:** **Alvin J. Fellows** (of New Haven, Connecticut) improved Bangs' design with two key features: a locking mechanism that holds the tape in place until a button is pressed, and an all-in-one compact metal case. His design was the first to closely resemble the common modern handyman's tool.
* **January 3, 1922:** **Hiram A. Farrand** patented the concave-convex tape. This design allowed the metal tape to remain rigid when extended and serves as the basis for most modern pocket tape measures.
* **1954:** **Ture Anders Ljungberg** introduced the **TALmeter**, an improved version featuring specialized scales and edges for transferring measurements.
* **March 1963:** **Stanley Tools** introduced the **PowerLock** series, the first to feature a molded ABS case, a thumb-actuated tape lock, and a riveted end hook.
* **1995:** **Starrett** released the first commercialized digital tape measure (DigiTape).
