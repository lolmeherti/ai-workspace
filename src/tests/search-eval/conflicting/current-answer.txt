According to a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (Source: PMC9589144), whether dark chocolate is "healthy" or "unhealthy" depends on its nutritional composition and the amount consumed. The research indicates that while it offers significant cardiovascular benefits, these can be offset by other nutritional factors.

### **Potential Health Benefits**
The research suggests that dark chocolate can have positive effects on cardiovascular health, primarily driven by the presence of **cocoa flavanols**. Key benefits include:
* **Blood Pressure Reduction:** A significant reduction in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure.
* **Improved Lipid Profile:** Improvements in cholesterol levels, specifically by increasing HDL ("good" cholesterol) and potentially decreasing LDL ("bad" cholesterol).
* **Enhanced Endothelial Function:** Improved ability of the blood vessels to dilate.

### **Risks and Considerations**
The research highlights several factors that can negate these benefits or lead to negative health outcomes:
* **Nutritional Composition:** Many commercial dark chocolates contain high levels of **sugar and saturated fats**, which can offset the cardiovascular benefits provided by the flavanols.
* **Caloric Intake:** Dark chocolate is energy-dense, meaning excessive consumption can lead to **weight gain**.
* **Flavanol Concentration:** The therapeutic effects are highly dependent on the concentration of flavanols; therefore, the specific type and quality of chocolate matter significantly.

**Summary:** Dark chocolate can be healthy for cardiovascular function due to its flavanol content, but it can become unhealthy if it is high in sugar and fat or consumed in quantities that lead to excessive calorie intake.
