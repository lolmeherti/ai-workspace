On July 19, 2024, a massive global IT outage occurred, primarily affecting Microsoft Windows systems. The disruption was caused by a faulty configuration update distributed by the cybersecurity firm **CrowdStrike** for its **Falcon Sensor** software.

The following is a detailed breakdown of the event based on the provided search results:

### **Technical Root Cause**
The outage was triggered by a configuration update intended to improve how the Falcon Sensor evaluated **named pipe execution** on Microsoft Windows. 
* **The Flaw:** A modification to "Channel File 291" caused an out-of-bounds memory read, leading to an invalid page fault.
* **Severity:** Because the update operates at the **kernel level** (the core of the operating system), the error had a profound impact on system stability.
* **Symptoms:** Affected systems experienced the "Blue Screen of Death" (BSOD), entered bootloops, or were forced into recovery mode.
* **Version Specificity:** The bug was present in versions with the timestamp **2024-07-19 0409 UTC**. Versions timestamped **2024-07-19 0527 UTC** or later were not affected.

### **Scope and Global Impact**
While initial reports estimated approximately 8.5 million affected Windows systems, Microsoft later clarified that the actual number of impacted devices is likely significantly higher. The outage primarily affected large organizations using Windows 10 and 11, while macOS, Linux, and most personal PCs remained unaffected.

The disruption paralyzed several critical sectors:
* **Aviation:** Delta Air Lines was severely hit, with over 37,000 computers affected, disrupting 1.3 million passengers and resulting in losses exceeding $500 million.
* **Healthcare:** North American hospitals had to pause non-urgent visits, and the UK’s National Health Service (NHS) experienced paralysis in GP practices due to an inability to access medical records.
* **Other Sectors:** Significant disruptions were reported in banking, manufacturing, stock markets, and government services.
* **Financial Consequences:** Global economic losses are estimated in the tens of billions of dollars. CrowdStrike specifically faced an estimated $5.4 billion in losses, and its company valuation dropped by more than $20 billion following a nearly 25% decline in share price.

### **Cybersecurity Context**
CrowdStrike confirmed that the outage was **not the result of a cyberattack**. However, threat actors exploited the chaos through several secondary methods:
* **Phishing and Impersonation:** Malicious actors used brand impersonation and phishing emails to target victims.
* **Malicious Files:** Distribution of malicious ZIP archives occurred, specifically targeting customers in Latin America.
* **The Handala Hacking Team:** This group posed as CrowdStrike support via phone calls and phishing emails approximately two weeks after the outage.

### **Remediation and Recovery**
Recovery efforts required a combination of automated tools and manual intervention:
* **CrowdStrike & Microsoft Actions:** CrowdStrike reverted the faulty update, and Microsoft released a USB-based recovery tool to help repair affected systems.
* **Manual Fix:** Because many systems were stuck in bootloops, technical staff often had to manually boot into Safe Mode or the Windows Recovery Environment to delete specific `.sys` files (starting with `C-00000291-`) located in the `%windir%\System32\drivers\CrowdStrike\` directory.
* **Complications:** Recovery was further complicated for systems using **BitLocker** encryption, which required the manual entry of 48-digit recovery keys—a difficult task if those keys were stored on servers that had also crashed.
